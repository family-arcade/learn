The first version is basic. Play it, then ask for two or three changes at a
time. That is enough to notice a difference after each merge. More at once
is hard to check.

After each version, Claude suggests two or three ideas. Your child can pick
one, or ask for something else.

## Next asks to try

<Prompt>Make the dragon flap its wings and bob a little while it flies. Play a happy sound when you catch a gem.</Prompt>

<Prompt>When you collect 5 gems in a row, show a little burst of sparkles. Add thunderclouds to dodge.</Prompt>

<Prompt>Add a level 2 where the thunderclouds move faster. Show which level you are on.</Prompt>

## Ways to ask

**Say what you see** after you play:

<Prompt>The dragon is too fast, and I can't see the gems on a phone.</Prompt>

**Say how it should feel,** not how to code it:

<Prompt>Make the flying feel bouncier.</Prompt>

**Ask for a test** when something must keep working. A test is a small check
that Claude runs after every change.

<Prompt>Write a test that touching a thundercloud ends the round.</Prompt>

**Keep the old version** when you are not sure about a change. Do not merge
it, and your game stays as it was.

**Remember the merge.** A change only goes live after you merge it.

## Adding pictures and sounds

Claude can draw shapes, characters and backgrounds in code. These load fast.
If your child draws something on paper, take a photo and give it to Claude:

<Prompt>Make the dragon look like this drawing.</Prompt>
