Your first game will be basic: a few shapes and one thing to do. That way it
works quickly and your child sees real results. Seeing them is what makes
children want to build the next, bigger version.

:::note[For parents]
Seeing their own game work within minutes is a big motivator for a child.
A basic game is also quick to build, easy to follow and easy to fix.
[Keeping the first game basic](/parents/#keeping-the-first-game-basic)
:::

## Example first games

We made each of these games in a fresh copy of the starter template. We
typed one sentence into Claude Code. Claude asked its questions, and we
answered each one with "You choose." Nothing was changed afterwards. The
time runs from the first message until Claude said the game was done. Your
child's answers will add a few minutes.

<FirstGames />

## Tell Claude your idea

Your child says the idea in one sentence. The grown-up types it, or your
child does.

<Prompt>Make a game where you are a dragon collecting gems in the clouds.</Prompt>

Claude then asks a few short questions, one at a time. Who are you in the
game? How do you win? Which colours? Let your child answer each one. If they
are stuck, Claude offers two or three choices.

## Let your child talk

Your child can say the words instead of typing them. Claude Code on the web
has no voice mode of its own. Your device can turn speech into text in any
text box, including Claude's:

- **On an iPad or iPhone,** tap the microphone key on the keyboard, then
  talk.
- **On a Mac,** turn on Dictation once, in System Settings under
  **Keyboard**. Then press the dictation shortcut and talk.

The words appear in the box. The grown-up checks them and sends them.

To talk the idea through before you build, use voice mode in the Claude app.
It is an ordinary chat, separate from Claude Code. It works best on a phone.
It runs on the grown-up's account, so stay with your child while they talk.
Tap the sound-wave icon next to the chat box to start. At the end, ask Claude
to write the game idea as one short paragraph. Paste that paragraph into
Claude Code.

## While Claude works

In our tests, Claude took four to six minutes for a first game. You can
watch it work:

- **It edits the game's files** and shows you each change.
- **It runs the tests,** small checks that the game still works. A test can
  fail. Claude then fixes the problem and runs them again. That is normal.
- **It may ask before it does something.** Read the question together.
  Answer it, and Claude carries on.
- **It says when it is done,** and what it changed. A **Create PR** button
  appears.

## Look at the change

Read what Claude changed, together. Is it what your child asked for? If
something is not right yet, say so before you merge:

<Prompt>The dragon is too small. Make it bigger.</Prompt>

Claude changes it on the same branch.

## Merge the change

A pull request asks to add the change to your game. The merge adds it.

1. In Claude, click **Create PR**. GitHub opens the pull request.
2. Merge the change:
   1. Click **Merge pull request**.
   2. Click **Confirm merge**.

Your first game is now in your repository. It is not on the web yet. The
next page puts it there, once, and then anyone can play it.
