## A basic game in an afternoon

The first game is basic on purpose: a few shapes and one thing to do. You
make it better from there.

<Steps>

1. **Start from the template.** It becomes your game's own repository, its
   home on GitHub.

2. **Tell Claude your idea in one sentence.** Claude asks your child a few
   questions, then makes a basic first version.

   <Prompt>Make a game where you are a dragon collecting gems in the clouds.</Prompt>

3. **Put it on the web.** One setting, and your game has its own address.

4. **Make it better,** two or three changes at a time. Play after every
   change.

5. **Play it with friends** in the Family Arcade, from their own homes.

</Steps>

Claude makes each change on a copy of your game, called a branch. Your game
changes only when you merge, with one button on GitHub.

## Playing together in the Family Arcade

Friends add your game to their own Family Arcade at
[familyarcade.eu](https://familyarcade.eu) and open it there with their own
player. One device shows four letters, and the others type them.

## Getting started needs three things

- **An hour,** and a computer for the first time.
- **A free GitHub account.** It keeps your game's code and puts it on the
  web. For a child under 13, it is a grown-up's account.
- **An AI helper.** This guide uses Claude Code, part of a paid Claude plan
  held by a grown-up. [Free options for teenagers](/make/) are listed too.

## Safe by design

- **No ads and no tracking,** in the arcade and in every game made from the
  starter template.
- **Only people with your code** can play with your child. There is no chat
  and no public list of players.
- **Players and saves stay on your device.** There are no accounts to sign up
  for.
