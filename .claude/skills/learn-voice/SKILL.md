# Learn voice

The readers are a child and a grown-up learning together, often reading aloud. The voice is a
**bright guide**: as clear as a good teacher, with a little wonder and real celebration of what
the child just achieved (the owner's pick, 2026-10-06). The point of every page is that the child
**sees results**: progress they can see makes them want to make the next, harder thing.

## What it sounds like

- **Clear first.** Point first, one idea per sentence, short sentences, words a child knows. Explain a
  new word the first time, in a few words.
- **A little spark.** One playful image or moment of wonder per section, at most. Specific praise that
  names what went right ("Every key landed"), never empty cheering ("Great job!!").
- **Honest and accurate.** Every promise must be true today. Say what really happens, and never
  anything you wouldn't say to the child's face: no "broken on purpose", no "tiny", no hype.
- **Progress is the reason.** Explain a step by what the child will see next: the game starts basic
  so it works quickly and they see results, and that's what makes them want to build more.
- **Mistakes are something to look at again**, never something to laugh at or scold. Say what to
  check and the next step.
- **Adults get the calm, competent voice** (German: "Sie"), with the same clarity and no spark.

## The shapes (budgets are maximums)

| Element | Shape | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Page or section intro | What the reader will make or learn, then why it matters to them | 2 sentences, 40 words |
| Step | One action, the exact button or key, verb first | 15 words |
| Instruction in a lesson | What to do now, then what to do if it goes wrong | 2 short sentences |
| Praise | Name what went right, specifically; one exclamation mark at most | 8 words |
| Near miss | What almost happened, then the next try | 12 words |
| Error | What happened in plain words, then what to check or do | 2 sentences, 25 words |
| Parent note | The fact, then what it means for their child | 2 sentences, 40 words |

## Write it in this order

1. **What will the child see or be able to do** after this? That's the first sentence.
2. **The steps**, one action each.
3. **Cut:** hedges, filler ("simply", "just", "easily"), and any line that explains instead of shows.
4. **Check every claim** against what the product really does today.
5. **Read it aloud** to an eight-year-old: they should know what to do and want to do it.
6. **Only now, the spark:** does this section earn one playful image or celebration? At most one.

## German (TippMitGalli)

The same voice in German: "du" to children, "Sie" to adults. Galli stays the tone test (curious,
precise, encouraging, never babyish). TippMitGalli's copy test still applies: at most one "!" per
string, no "?!", no "…", no filler words. The owner doesn't read German, so the TippMitGalli session
checks the German itself and shows the owner the English meaning of each changed line.

Before and after: `examples.md`. Games use the arcade voice instead.
